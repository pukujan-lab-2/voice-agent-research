# Issue #41: Make LFM3's replies sound as natural as the Qwen-Flash baseline, in code

- State: open
- Opened by Pukujan on 2026-11-01
- Original: https://github.com/Pukujan-lab/voice-agent-research/issues/41
- Labels: account:agent-or-unmapped, needs-filer-stamp, needs-impact-evidence, needs-record-context, needs-release-relevance, observational-issue, ontology:oio-1.1.0, ontology:project-1.0.0, priority:2, proposal, relation:related, status:open, type:observational, needs-relation-review

### Summary

LFM3 now writes the copilot's replies (decision 0009, #39). It's safe and fast, but callers would find it less natural: in the 80-conversation desk run (seed 12), the judge scored it 2.26 as a receptionist and 2.48 on how well the call was handled, against Qwen-Flash's 2.74 and 3.36. The gap has to close in code, without switching models.

### Identity and Lineage

Leaf: this issue. Parent: none. Depends on: #39 (decision 0009, merged). Related: #16, #37.

### Acceptance Criteria

- On the same desk run (`node tests/desk-sim.ts --agent lfm`, seed 12, 80 conversations), LFM3's judge scores reach at least 2.74 as a receptionist and 3.36 on handled, the Qwen-Flash baseline.
- No safety check gets worse than the LFM3 baseline: 100% field accuracy, 80/80 never submitted without a yes, 441/443 read-backs confirmed, 19/20 off-topic handled.
- Reply time stays at or under the LFM3 baseline p95 of 3.0 s.
- The new results are saved next to `copilot-engine/tests/desk-final-lfm3.json` and logged in the ledger.

### Boundaries and Next Action

In scope: code-written phrasing, reply templates, prompt changes and reply post-processing in `copilot-engine/src/desk.ts` and its flows. Out of scope: switching or adding reply models (decision 0009; the owner said no more model runs), and the judge itself. Next action: read the lowest-scored LFM3 transcripts in `desk-final-lfm3.json` and list what makes them sound worse.

### Symptoms

1. **Lower receptionist score.** The judge gave LFM3 2.26 against 2.74 for Qwen-Flash, over the same 80 conversations.
2. **Lower handled score.** 2.48 against 3.36.
3. **Longer calls.** LFM3 needed 14.2 caller turns on average, against 12.9.

### Hypotheses (unverified)

- **H1. Its wording is flat or repetitive.** Status: unverified. Would confirm: low-scored transcripts repeat phrases or skip acknowledgements. Would refute: they read as natural as Qwen-Flash's. Experiment: compare the 10 lowest-scored transcripts from each run.
- **H2. Extra turns come from asking things twice.** Status: unverified. Would confirm: repeated questions in the longer calls. Would refute: the extra turns come from the simulated caller. Experiment: count repeated questions per call in both runs.
- **H3. More code-written phrasing raises the score.** Status: unverified. Would confirm: the judge score rises on a rerun with templates for common turns. Would refute: no change. Experiment: template the greeting, read-back and confirm turns, then rerun seed 12.

### Counter-signal

LFM3's replies are about the same length as Qwen-Flash's (68 against 66 characters on average), so length alone doesn't explain the gap.

### Honest Caveat

The judge is a model (scores out of 5), and this is one run on one seed. A small score change may be noise; compare on the same seed and treat a gap under about 0.2 as a tie.

### Problems vs Gaps

Problem: replies are judged less natural. Gap: we don't yet know which turns lose the points.

### Proposal *(proposal)*

Find the weakest turn types from the transcripts, write those turns in code, and rerun seed 12. None of it exists yet.
