The live site read back every field and waited for a yes, so a time slot took as long as spelling a name, and nothing let a business mark a field as one a person must approve.

## What changes

- **Each field is confirmed by its risk score from 1 to 100**, as decision 0011 (#53) sets out:
  - up to 30: filled at once and said in passing ("Okay, Morning for the time of day.");
  - 31 to 55: read back, then a yes;
  - 56 to 75: code holds the value until a clear yes, and "sure, whatever" or "I guess" doesn't count;
  - 76 to 90: after the yes it asks "are you sure?", and a skip is asked twice too;
  - 91 and up: never filled; the caller hears that a person on the team approves it.
- **Defaults when a page sets no score:** a pick from a short list and an appointment time score 25, typed values 40, secret fields (date of birth, last four digits) 60. A page can set its own with `data-copilot-risk` on a field or fieldset.
- **A mishearing raises the stakes.** After the caller rejects a read-back or corrects a value, every remaining field scores 15 higher for the rest of the call, so short picks are read back again.
- **The bands live in one place** (`RISK_BANDS` in `desk.ts`). The prompt prints each field's score and rule from it, and the code lock reads the same numbers.
- **Every fill goes to Phoenix** as a `confirm` span with its field, score, read-back and the caller's yes (or "in passing"), so evals can check that every fill from 56 up had its yes. The value stays out of the span's attributes; the masked read-back carries it.
- **Fixes the live LFM3 runs turned up:**
  - when LFM3 names a choice badly ("S" for "my son", or the range above the one said), the option the caller's own words name wins;
  - "the name should be Leo Ray" goes to the one filled name field even when LFM3 only opens the field;
  - a proposal for a field that isn't open, made from words the caller never said, is dropped without a "say that again", and no longer stops the open field's answer from being filled.
